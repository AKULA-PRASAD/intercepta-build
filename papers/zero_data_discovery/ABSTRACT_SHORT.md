When a new or neglected pathogen — or any disease — presents with **zero activity data**, how far can computation get toward
credible intervention targets from sequence, structure, and transferable knowledge alone, and where does it break? We built
pre-registered, reproduced-twice, open-data experiments that map this frontier honestly. For target identification we establish
a well-controlled negative (homology does not beat a conservation null and can fail silently) and the one signal that breaks the
ceiling: mechanistic **flux-balance gene-essentiality**, enriched for *experimental* gene-knockout essentiality across six
curated metabolic models (odds ratios 5–64). An **analyst-blind (lock-before-reveal) suite across all three domains of life**
reveals a sharp, honest **prokaryote/eukaryote split (4 pass / 3 fail)**: every prokaryote with an adequate model passes — three
bacterial phyla and an **archaeon** — while failures fall *predictably* on invariant/model boundaries. Predicting on a never-seen
domain yet failing coherently along biology is the signature of generalization, not memorization.

We formalize a **transfer-condition principle**: each label-free signal transfers only as far as the biological invariant it
rides on is conserved. Viruses: sequence homology fails, but blind **structural** homology recovers the correct drugged-enzyme
class across five viruses. Human cancer: a **functional-dependency** signal recovers known targets and generalizes to held-out
lines. Load-bearing **negatives** bound the work — dependency does not transfer to a zero-screen organism; patient drug-response
prediction fails external replication; and a pre-registered attempt to make the transfer principle an *a-priori* law fails (it
stays qualitative). The validated signals compose into a **biology-class-aware router** that applies what is validated and
**abstains** otherwise — "any disease" as honest decision coverage, not a universal model. We then close the molecule/affinity
half **at power**: state-of-the-art **co-folding** (Boltz-2, 522 GPU complexes) fails a leakage-controlled novel-chemotype
split, underperforming a fingerprint QSAR. No claim is clinical; the remaining distance to a drug is gated by **new experimental
information, not more computation**.

## 1. Why this paper matters for Q7

Reynolds & Pan 2025 is **the only published systematic benchmark of feature attribution methods on predictive genomics with synthetic ground truth.** Three reasons it matters for INTERCEPTA:

1. **It quantifies which attribution methods actually work** — the question every Decision 7 v2 architecture must answer empirically. Prior to this paper, the field used IG/SHAP/DeepLIFT/Saliency interchangeably; Reynolds & Pan provides the rigorous comparison.

2. **It establishes SmoothGrad as a cheap-but-decisive improvement** across all four attribution algorithms. SmoothGrad is operationally a few lines of code; the precision improvement (+0.06 at top 1%) and recall improvement (+0.16 at top 1%) are large enough that **not using SmoothGrad in INTERCEPTA's Q7 layer would be a methodological choice that fails the audit.**

3. **It introduces synthetic-spike-in + null-decoy methodology** that enables direct precision/recall measurement. INTERCEPTA can adapt this methodology to evaluate its own Q7 layer on its own data — providing a falsifiable validation framework for Charter §1.3 I1 (gene-level attribution).

## 2. What they did — full methodology

### 2.1 Training infrastructure

**Dataset:** UK Biobank (the largest population genomics resource available)
- ~300,000 participants (post-quality-control)
- >500,000 autosomal variants
- Phenotype: standing height (continuous, polygenic, well-characterized)
- Quality control: standard UK Biobank protocols (call rate, MAF, HWE, kinship filtering)

**Models:** Feed-forward neural networks trained on genotype → height
- Multiple independent training runs for ensemble stability analysis
- Standard regression objective (MSE on height)
- Held-out test set for performance evaluation

### 2.2 The four attribution algorithms benchmarked

**Saliency (gradient × input):**
- Simplest gradient-based attribution
- Per-variant gradient of prediction w.r.t. input genotype
- Computationally cheapest

**Gradient SHAP:**
- Stochastic SHAP approximation using gradients
- Multiple reference baselines
- Compromise between SHAP rigor and gradient efficiency

**DeepLIFT:**
- Difference-from-reference attribution
- Propagates contribution differences backward through network
- Chain-rule-respecting

**Integrated Gradients (IG):**
- Path integral from baseline to input
- Theoretically satisfies completeness + sensitivity axioms
- Computational cost ~50 forward+backward passes per attribution

### 2.3 SmoothGrad noise averaging

For each of the 4 algorithms, evaluated **with and without** SmoothGrad:
- Inject Gaussian noise to input genotype N times
- Average the attributions across noisy versions
- Reduces noise-amplified gradient artifacts

**This produces 8 algorithm-configurations total** (4 base × 2 SmoothGrad on/off).

### 2.4 Three-aspect benchmarking framework

The paper introduces **three quantitative aspects of interpretability** measured per algorithm-configuration:

**Aspect 1 — Attribution Recall:**
- **Synthetic spike-in variants** with known effects injected into the data
- Effect types tested: additive, dominant, recessive, epistatic
- Measure: what fraction of true causal variants does the attribution method rank in the top X%?
- Enables **direct sensitivity measurement** across diverse genetic architectures

**Aspect 2 — Attribution Precision:**
- **Null decoy variants** generated by permuting sample indices within genotype subsets
- Preserves per-variant allele frequency distribution
- Disrupts genotype-phenotype correspondence
- Measure: at top X% threshold, what fraction of "important" variants are real (not decoys)?
- **Equivalent to specificity** — controls for false positives

**Aspect 3 — Stability:**
- Train ensemble of independently initialized models
- Measure: consistency of variant-level attributions across ensemble members
- Quantified by median relative standard deviation
- **Decision 5 v2 N=5 Deep Ensembles directly supports this measurement** — operational integration

### 2.5 Decoy variant construction (methodologically critical)

The decoy methodology is the **key novel contribution** for falsifiable benchmarking. Per the paper:
- Permute sample indices within each genotype subset
- Use deterministic chunk-aware random seed for reproducibility
- Apply distinct seeds to train/val/test splits to ensure independence
- Resulting decoys preserve allele structure but break genotype-phenotype correspondence

**For INTERCEPTA:** this methodology directly applies. INTERCEPTA can construct decoy cells (cells from non-perturbation conditions labeled with random perturbation labels) and decoy drugs (drugs with permuted target annotations) to measure Q7 attribution precision falsifiably.

## 3. What they found — quantitative results

### 3.1 SmoothGrad is the decisive improvement

**Across all four base algorithms, SmoothGrad consistently improves performance:**
- **Average recall increase: +0.16** at top 1% of most attributed variants (across effect types)
- **Average precision increase: +0.06** at top 1% threshold
- **Stability remains comparable** — SmoothGrad doesn't hurt consistency
- **Median relative standard deviation** in attribution values across ensemble: maintained

This is the headline operational finding. **SmoothGrad is a cheap, universally beneficial preprocessing step** — INTERCEPTA's Decision 7 v2 must include it.

### 3.2 Algorithm-specific precision findings (from primary source)

From the existing note's primary-source-verified numbers (also supported by PMC text):
- **Saliency + SmoothGrad:** precision ~0.36 (20% threshold), ~0.51 (10% threshold)
- **Integrated Gradients + SmoothGrad:** precision ~0.75 at 2% threshold (highest at strict thresholds)
- **Gradient SHAP + SmoothGrad:** precision ~0.68 at 3% threshold
- **Non-smoothed variants:** ~0.25-0.38 precision at 10-20% thresholds (substantially worse)

**Operational ranking:** for strict top-1-3% gene selection (INTERCEPTA's likely use case for clinical drug target nomination), IG+SmoothGrad > Gradient SHAP+SmoothGrad > DeepLIFT+SmoothGrad > Saliency+SmoothGrad.

### 3.3 Recall by genetic architecture

The synthetic spike-in design enables stratified recall measurement:
- **Additive effects:** highest recall (linear DNN can capture)
- **Dominant effects:** strong recall (well-handled by DNNs)
- **Recessive effects:** moderate recall (non-linear)
- **Epistatic effects:** lowest recall (highest-order non-linearity)

**For INTERCEPTA:** drug response prediction often involves epistatic effects (gene-gene-drug interactions). The Reynolds-Pan finding that epistatic recall is lowest is a **warning** — INTERCEPTA's Q7 layer must be evaluated specifically on epistatic-like scenarios in drug response context.

### 3.4 Stability vs algorithm choice

Algorithm choice has **less impact on stability than SmoothGrad** does on precision/recall. This means the architectural priority is: SmoothGrad first, algorithm second. **INTERCEPTA can use any of the 4 algorithms with SmoothGrad** without losing stability — algorithm choice can be optimized for precision/recall trade-offs per use case.

## 4. What's strong

- **PLOS Computational Biology peer-reviewed** — top methodology venue (IF ~6, but methodology-focused; respected in computational biology)
- **Synthetic spike-in + null decoy methodology** — the gold standard for attribution benchmarking; enables true precision/recall measurement
- **UK Biobank scale (~300K participants, >500K variants)** — realistic data scale, not toy example
- **Four attribution algorithms × SmoothGrad on/off = 8 configurations** — comprehensive coverage
- **Three benchmarking aspects (recall, precision, stability)** — multidimensional evaluation
- **Effect-type stratification (additive/dominant/recessive/epistatic)** — biologically meaningful decomposition
- **Ensemble-based stability** — methodologically rigorous; INTERCEPTA's Decision 5 v2 N=5 Deep Ensembles natively supports this
- **2025 recent publication** — reflects current best practices
- **CC BY open access** — INTERCEPTA can adopt methodology and code freely
- **Reproducible decoy methodology** — deterministic seeding documented

## 5. What's limited

- **Genotype-phenotype focus, NOT gene expression-drug response.** Continuous height phenotype from tabular SNP data — different problem structure from INTERCEPTA's scRNA-seq drug response prediction. **Transfer of conclusions to INTERCEPTA's context requires empirical verification.**
- **Continuous trait (height) — may differ for discrete drug response classes.** Classification problems may have different attribution behavior than regression.
- **Feed-forward DNNs on tabular data — different architecture from CPA/chemCPA/GEARS.** Whether the attribution algorithm rankings transfer to VAE / GNN / Transformer architectures is unclear.
- **No comparison with non-gradient methods** — Shapley value-based DeepSHAP, LIME, or model-agnostic methods not included.
- **No biological discovery validation** — the paper measures recall/precision/stability but does not validate that the recovered features are biologically meaningful (unlike EIG 2020 which discovered A1CF).
- **SmoothGrad hyperparameter (noise scale, N samples)** — not deeply ablated; defaults used.
- **Polygenic trait (height) with strong common-variant signal** — may not extrapolate to rare-variant or pathway-driven phenotypes.
- **No path-integrated alternatives tested beyond IG** (e.g., Expected Gradients, BlurIG).

## 6. INTERCEPTA implications

### For Decision 7 v2 (mechanistic interpretability)

Reynolds & Pan 2025 establishes **three binding operational facts for Decision 7 v2:**

1. **SmoothGrad must be applied to all attribution methods** in INTERCEPTA's Q7 stack. Not using SmoothGrad costs ~0.16 recall at top 1% — a 50%+ relative degradation. There is no architectural reason to skip it.

2. **Integrated Gradients + SmoothGrad is the highest-precision option for strict thresholds** (top 2-3% of genes). For INTERCEPTA's clinical drug target nomination, this is the operational use case — IG+SmoothGrad should be the primary attribution method.

3. **Gradient SHAP + SmoothGrad is the strongest secondary** for moderate thresholds (top 3-5%) — pair with IG for cross-method consensus.

### For Decision 5 v2 (OOD detection) integration

Reynolds-Pan stability methodology uses **independently trained model ensembles** — operationally identical to Decision 5 v2 Layer 5.2 Deep Ensembles N=5. **INTERCEPTA's Q5 stack natively provides the ensemble that Q7 stability measurement requires.** Decision 7 v2 must specify that the same N=5 ensemble serves both purposes.

### For Decision 4 v2 (drug response architecture) integration

Decision 4 v2 Slot 5 (mode collapse mitigation) requires **diversity ≥ 50% of training data**. Reynolds-Pan's stability metric (median relative standard deviation across ensemble) is a candidate diversity quantification mechanism. **Decision 7 v2 can co-bind with Decision 4 v2 Slot 5 via the same stability methodology.**

### For Decision 6 v2 (validation cascade)

Reynolds-Pan's decoy methodology provides the **falsifiable evaluation framework** for INTERCEPTA's Q7 layer. Decision 6 v2 V0-V6 pass criteria can include Q7 attribution precision targets per the Reynolds-Pan methodology. **For INTERCEPTA:** construct decoy cells (random perturbation labels) and decoy drugs (permuted target annotations) to measure Q7 precision/recall.

### For Charter §1.3 I1 (gene-level attribution)

Reynolds-Pan establishes the **empirical methodology** for I1. INTERCEPTA's I1 layer must:
- Use IG+SmoothGrad (or Gradient SHAP+SmoothGrad as alternative)
- Measure recall + precision + stability per the Reynolds-Pan framework
- Stratify by effect type (additive vs epistatic) — epistatic recall is the hardest case
- Report stability across Decision 5 v2 N=5 ensemble

### For Decision 1 v2 (substrate flexibility) interaction

If Decision 1 v2's Layer 5 ablation selects parameter-free substrate (Souza & Mehta scTOP), **gene-level attribution becomes architecturally easier** — linear projections expose gene coefficients directly. If FM substrate wins, IG+SmoothGrad over FM input layer is operationally feasible but more expensive. **Decision 7 v2 must specify both branches**: gene attribution for parameter-free; IG+SmoothGrad over FM input for FM substrate.

### Critical methodological lesson

Reynolds-Pan demonstrates **that proper benchmarking requires synthetic ground truth.** INTERCEPTA cannot publish Q7 layer claims without similar synthetic ground truth methodology. **The decoy construction is the methodologically critical piece** — INTERCEPTA must adapt this to drug response context.
